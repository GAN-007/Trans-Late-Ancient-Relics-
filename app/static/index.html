From image, voice or text to a traceable linguistic analysis

Use the camera on an inscription, speak a phrase, type English or Swahili, inspect multiple translation candidates, hear readable output, and learn why a translation is uncertain instead of receiving a single unexplained answer.

◉

Live camera translation

Explicit camera permission, frame-by-frame analysis, reading direction, sign candidates and uncertainty. Cloud vision is opt-in and only runs after you start it.

◌

Voice and read-aloud

Use device speech recognition where available, send recognized English/Swahili text through the translation engine, and read results aloud with browser speech synthesis.

◇

Context and ambiguity

Translations carry confidence, rationale, literal meaning, alternatives, assumptions and provenance so nuance is visible rather than silently collapsed.

𓏭

ESHB reversible bridge

English, Swahili and IPA can be encoded into a modern reversible hieroglyphic bridge without pretending the result is historical Middle Egyptian.

𓏤

Ancient Egyptian learning

Twenty-six structured Middle Egyptian lessons, vocabulary practice, sign tables, transliteration, MdC and a growing reviewed dictionary.

△

Human-reviewed AI growth

Repeated unknown terms and user feedback form a research queue. AI can suggest entries, but reviewers must approve them before they join the learner lexicon.

Design principles
  • Camera and microphone are permission-gated and user-started.
  • Deterministic ESHB and dictionary features remain useful without AI.
  • AI suggestions are secondary evidence, not silently authoritative.
  • Historical pronunciation is distinguished from classroom/readable pronunciation.
  • Mobile, tablet and desktop share the same API and responsive interface.
